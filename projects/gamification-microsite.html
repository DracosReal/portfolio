Gamification in Learning: Educational Microsite

A multi-page educational microsite for a graduate instructional design course at Arizona State University, researching, synthesizing, and presenting gamification theory, cognitivism, serious games, and their application in learning environments.

Role
Research, UX writing, web design
Team
Solo project
Timeline
Summer 2024, ASU LDT 501
Tools
Google Sites, research synthesis, instructional design
Visit the microsite

The brief

Gamification is frequently misunderstood: conflated with game-based learning, reduced to points and badges, or dismissed as a gimmick.

The assignment called for a research-grounded educational resource that could explain the concept accurately, distinguish it from adjacent approaches, and make the case for its legitimate role in instructional design.

The structure

The microsite is organized as a layered argument: it starts with a definition and rationale, moves through the theoretical underpinning in cognitivism and serious games, then draws a clear distinction between serious games and gamification with an honest pros and cons analysis.

Minecraft Education Edition is used as a grounded case study, a recognizable, well-documented example that bridges game mechanics and educational application. All claims are cited from instructional design literature.

What it covers

  • What is Gamification? A definition, core mechanics (points, badges, leaderboards, scenario-based learning, adaptive game-based learning), and the impact on instructional design practice
  • Cognitivism and Serious Games: the theoretical grounding, with Minecraft Education Edition as a detailed case study in applied game-based learning
  • Serious Games vs. Gamification: a clear distinction between the two approaches, with a documented pros and cons analysis for each
  • Resources and References: full citation of the instructional design literature behind the content
  • Three organizing principles that carry the site's argument: Gamification Gives Purpose, Gamification Scales, and Gamification is Formative
