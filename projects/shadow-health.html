Shadow Health: Virtual Patient Design

A narrative design interview challenge for a healthcare simulation platform: clinically accurate virtual patients, authentic dialogue, and instructional social context content for nursing and medical training.

Role
Narrative designer, dialogue writer, instructional content designer
Team
Solo project (interview challenge)
Timeline
2 to 3 days, 2022
Tools
Primary research, clinical literature, APA citation

The challenge

Healthcare simulation requires characters who feel human enough to train empathy, while remaining clinically precise enough to teach diagnosis. The dialogue has to carry emotional subtext (a patient's deflection, anxiety, or humor) while surfacing the medical information a student needs to identify.

Get the voice wrong and students learn nothing. Get the medicine wrong and it's worse.

The method

Each character was built from the intersection of clinical data and lived specificity.

For Edna, a 74-year-old with a UTI, that meant interviewing family members and grounding her voice in the particular way older women minimize their own discomfort to avoid burdening people they love.

For Lucas, a patient in a manic episode, it meant finding the internal logic of pressured speech and clang associations: humor that makes sense from inside the episode even as it signals distress.

For Seamus, a character I originated, it meant layering hereditary hemochromatosis, Irish-American cultural identity, and non-binary experience into a case study that asked nursing students to hold all three at once.

From the dialogue

Two of the three patients, in their own words. Seamus and the social context slides are in the full challenge.

Lucas Callahan

Bipolar disorder, manic episode: a health and safety assessment with a patient experiencing pressured speech and clang associations.

Can you tell me about your family history?
Well, I don't know anything about my birth parents. But my actual parents are super healthy. They go for, like, jogs on the beach while talking about what sort of flaxseed goes best in kale smoothies. You can't take them anywhere.
How much do you weigh?
I weigh more than a sack of potatoes and less than a truck of potatoes... but not a scalloped potato. Oh, I'd love scalloped potatoes right now.
Is English your only language?
No. I also know the language of love. Don't you love, love? It's lovely.

Edna Smith

A 74-year-old retired schoolteacher presenting with UTI symptoms, widowed and living with her adult daughter's family.

Tell me about your symptoms.
I think I have another UTI... The other night I got a stomach ache. I thought maybe some indigestion, you know, at my age... but my back was in a knot. Then there were some bathroom issues, so I've been wearing one of those pads.
Tell me about your home life.
After my Charles moved on, I felt somewhat lonely. My daughter and her husband insisted I move in. I've always been independent, but they worry, and I don't like them to feel poorly. Being with my grandkids... Well, it helps a bit.
Do you ever feel fatigued?
[raises an eyebrow and laughs] Doesn't everyone?

Responses tied to learning goals

Each answer was written to a learning goal, so the conversation itself does the teaching.

Defensiveness about drinking tests whether a student can flag a risk without alienating the patient. A father's "liver trouble" and an uncle's "bronze skin" test whether a student recognizes a hereditary pattern.

What was delivered

  • Three fully voiced virtual patients: Lucas (bipolar manic episode), Edna (UTI, elderly care), and Seamus (hemochromatosis, non-binary, Irish-American, an original character)
  • Dialogue written in first person from inside each patient's perspective: clinically accurate, emotionally authentic, and a distinct voice for each
  • Three social context slides: pain management disparities in Black patients, HIV barriers for Vietnamese-American immigrants, and hereditary disease risk intersecting with non-binary mental health in Irish-American patients
  • All social context content researched and APA-cited from peer-reviewed sources, including the CDC, NIH, and Rheumatic Disease Clinics of North America
  • Research methods documented: primary interviews for Edna, and clinical literature for hemochromatosis prevalence and hereditary disease patterns
  • Delivered within 2 to 3 days as a competitive interview challenge
